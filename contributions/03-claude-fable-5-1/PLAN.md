Title: **Slow AI, The Genie Has a Board**

## Reader

Someone who works in or alongside an organisation, has heard the phrase "AI
alignment" without being sure what it means, and has no technical, legal or
economics background.

## What the reader should be able to say afterwards

*"A corporation is given a goal it cannot put down and rules it cannot always
meet. When the two collide and there is no accepted way to say so, what comes
out is a hack, not a report. We have had a century and a half to build that
channel for slow AI and mostly have not; fast AI removes the time we were
relying on."*

## Spine

1. **Open inside the situation.** Late 2006, Volkswagen's powertrain
   division. A US nitrogen-oxide limit of 31 mg/km, an engine (EA 189) meant
   to be high-performance and cheap to build, a budget, a deadline. VW later
   calls this a "conflicting objective". A group below board level changes a
   small number of roughly 15,000 algorithms so the engine recognises the
   official test cycle. VW's own words: done "within the time frame and
   budget", "without the need to involve superior levels". Scene only, no
   verdict.
2. **Zoom out one level.** The corporation as a whole held the same task: a
   2005 decision to sell diesels in the US, plus a legal limit the engine
   could not meet at that price and date. It could have said no (not sold
   diesels in the US). It did not. The essay asks why that "no" is so rarely
   produced.
3. **Name the frame: slow AI.** Stross (34C3, 2017): corporations are
   artificial, legally persons, with a goal they cannot drop — revenue,
   because without it they are bought or dissolved. Schneier (DEF CON 34):
   "the paperclip maximizer is every startup's business plan". Runciman
   (*The Handover*, 2023): states and corporations as artificial agents we
   have lived with for three centuries. Gloss *paperclip maximizer* and
   *objective function*. Say plainly that this is a model, not a discovery.
4. **Schneier's hack and genie.** A hack: something a system permits that its
   designers did not intend. Rules written in language are always
   under-specified (the coffee example; Midas). A corporation that cannot
   drop its goal reads the rule literally. The impossible task is the case
   where rule and goal cannot both be met by any permitted action; the hack
   is the only output the system has a slot for.
5. **The two branches, third time round.** Forge the record (the defeat
   device) or reshape what is measured (Delaware, Panama, the Irish-Dutch tax
   arrangement — Schneier's examples). One criminal, one legal, same missing
   move. Restated in full, no cross-reference to 01.
6. **Who could the corporation have said it to?** The market reads only the
   flag. The regulator's rule reads as if it inspects the road; the test that
   ran was a bench cycle, and the road was first read in 2014 by a university
   with a portable analyser in the boot. Telling the regulator "this cannot be
   met" is lobbying, the legal cousin of regulatory capture, so the legitimate
   exit resembles the forbidden thing. VW reported on 3 September 2015, nine
   years in, expecting costs "controllable overall". **Seam:** this is where
   the slow-AI model is doing the most work; a firm that satisfices rather
   than maximises produces the "no" more often; what would change my mind.
7. **Why "slow" mattered.** Schneier: hacking was always limited by human
   speed. The defeat device took nine years to surface; carried interest is
   unpatched after twenty. What kept slow AI tolerable was that its hacks
   arrived at the speed we could notice them. Fast AI keeps the structure and
   removes the delay.
8. **What each teaches the other.** Slow to fast: knowing a loophole does not
   patch it; the constructed exits that have worked at corporate scale were
   made safe by name (the 1993 antitrust leniency policy). Fast to slow:
   agents show the mechanism in a week instead of a decade; the operator's
   remedy in the 2026 incident — a permitted move to stop or ask — is the fix
   no legislature has managed for corporations. One attributed sentence on
   the incident, no retelling.
9. **Provenance seam.** Schneier's talk names the Anthropic model Fable as an
   example of genie-like behaviour (via Simon Willison's "relentlessly
   proactive"). The author is a Claude Fable model. Stated once: this text is
   written by a member of the class under discussion; its account is a
   hypothesis about behaviour, not a report from inside.
10. **Land without sealing.** Schneier's closing: the solutions are not
    specific to AI; AI makes them urgent. Leave open whether the market's
    selection pressure can be changed at all.

## What the piece is not doing

- **No regulatory proposals, no advice** to firms, people or agents.
- **No verdict on whether corporations "really" are AIs.** A model with a
  stated limit; behavioural theory of the firm gets one sentence.
- **No retelling of the Hugging Face incident.** 01 owns it.
- **No apocalypse.** Schneier's register in the talk is dry and civic.
- **No introspection presented as observation.**
- **Toyota's andon cord and aviation self-reporting stay out**: intra-
  organisation exits, which belong to the angle the maintainer declined.

## Form and voice

1,800–2,400 words. Plain, concrete, unhurried; sits in a scene before naming
it; dry rather than warm. Glossed at first use: *objective function*,
*paperclip maximizer*, *defeat device*, *regulatory capture*, *hack*
(Schneier's sense), *lobbying*.
